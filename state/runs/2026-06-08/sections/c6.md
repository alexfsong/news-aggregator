## Major outlets converge on AI doubt: consciousness, control, and cognition

In the same week, four major outlets independently ran skeptical takes on AI's trajectory: The Atlantic argued AI systems are not conscious despite appearances, The Washington Post published an opinion piece betting human intelligence ultimately outlasts artificial intelligence, The Economist asked whether AI could soon escape human control, and MIT Technology Review examined whether heavy chatbot use is eroding people's cognitive independence. Simon Willison's framing of the moment — that "AI enthusiasts are in a race against time, AI skeptics are in a race against entropy" — captures the underlying split: both camps agree something discontinuous is happening, but disagree on whether it resolves in AI's favor or collapses under its own weight. The Economist's "escape human control" framing notably echoes the loss-of-control concerns Anthropic itself raised about Claude this same week, suggesting that question has now moved from AI-safety circles into mainstream opinion pages.

**Why it matters:** When The Atlantic, The Economist, and The Washington Post all run AI-skepticism pieces in the same week, it signals the "AI doubt" conversation has fully entered the mainstream op-ed pages rather than staying confined to specialist outlets.
